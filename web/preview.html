A slower way
to see the coast

Some places are best understood on foot, with enough time to notice what sits between the landmarks.

We reached the harbour before the boats left. The water was still, and the path followed the shore past a row of empty benches. There was no particular destination to hurry towards.

Beyond the harbour, the road narrowed to a walking trail. Wild grass softened its edges. Every few minutes the sea disappeared behind the trees, then returned in a different shade of blue.

Leave room for a detour

The best part of the walk was not on our route. A small opening in the wall led down to a sheltered beach, where the only sound was water moving over stones.

We stayed until the light changed. By then, the tide had covered our first footprints, and the way back looked unfamiliar enough to feel like another walk.

What we took home

Not a list of places to return to, but a different sense of pace. Next time, we would start with fewer plans and leave the afternoon open.
